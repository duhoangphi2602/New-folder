# Embedded I/O Overview

## 1. Concept
In Embedded Systems, Input and Output are completely physical. You are reading the electrical voltage on a microscopic metal pin on the silicon chip (Input), or you are forcing a voltage onto that pin (Output).

## 2. Why
A PC is a closed box. An embedded system is designed specifically to talk to the physical world: reading temperature sensors, spinning motors, and talking to radios.

## 3. The Two Main Types of Embedded I/O

### 1. General Purpose Input/Output (GPIO)
This is the simplest form of I/O. You use a memory-mapped register to turn a physical pin High (3.3 Volts) or Low (0 Volts). 
- **Output:** Turn on an LED. Fire a relay.
- **Input:** Read a button press (Voltage goes from High to Low).

### 2. Serial Communication
If you need to send complex data (like a string or a 32-bit float), you cannot use 32 separate GPIO pins. Instead, you send the data sequentially, one bit at a time, over a single pin. This requires dedicated hardware peripherals inside the chip to manage the timing.
- **UART:** Asynchronous, 2 wires (TX/RX). Used for debugging and PC communication.
- **SPI:** Synchronous (has a clock), fast, 3-4 wires. Used for SD cards and LCD screens.
- **I2C:** Synchronous, slow, 2 wires. Used for simple sensors and EEPROM.

## 4. The "Console" Illusion
On a microcontroller, there is no screen. So where does `printf("Hello")` go?
It goes nowhere, unless you tell it to!
In embedded development, you write a custom function that takes the characters generated by `printf`, routes them to the chip's internal UART peripheral, and blasts them out of a TX (Transmit) pin. You then connect a "USB-to-TTL" cable from that pin to your laptop, open a terminal program (like PuTTY or TeraTerm), and view the data. 
*(This process, called "Redirecting", is covered in depth in Topic 06).*

## 6. The Execution Pipeline (PC vs MCU)

**PC Pipeline (`printf("Hi")`):**
1. `printf` writes to RAM buffer.
2. OS Kernel interrupts.
3. OS Video Driver converts text to pixel matrix.
4. GPU sends pixel data via HDMI to Monitor.

**Embedded Pipeline (`printf("Hi")`):**
1. `printf` formats string in RAM.
2. Developer's custom `_write()` hook is called.
3. `_write()` copies characters to the UART hardware Data Register (MMIO).
4. UART hardware shifts bits out of the physical TX pin at 115200 baud.
5. Laptop reads bits via USB and displays them.
