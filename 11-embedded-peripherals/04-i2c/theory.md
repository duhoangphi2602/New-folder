# I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit)

## 1. The 2-Wire Bus
I2C relies on exactly two wires, shared by all devices on the bus:
1. **SCL (Serial Clock):** Generated by the Master. Synchronizes the data.
2. **SDA (Serial Data):** A bi-directional line where data flows both ways (Half-Duplex).

## 2. The Open-Drain Requirement (CRITICAL)
In SPI, if two Slaves transmit at the same time (one outputs 3.3V, one outputs 0V), it creates a catastrophic short circuit. 
To safely allow 127 devices to share the single `SDA` wire, I2C *mandates* that all devices configure their pins as **Open-Drain**.
- No device is allowed to actively push 3.3V onto the wire.
- Both the SDA and SCL wires must be connected to 3.3V through external **Pull-Up Resistors** (usually 4.7kΩ).
- The resting state (Idle) of the bus is HIGH (`1`).
- To send a `0`, a device actively pulls the wire to GND.
- To send a `1`, the device simply disconnects from the wire (letting the resistor pull it back up to 3.3V).
*Result:* If two devices transmit simultaneously, the one sending `0` wins (it pulls the line low). No short circuit occurs.

## 3. The I2C Transaction
Because there are no Chip Select (CS) wires, the Master must shout the name of the Slave it wants to talk to over the data line.
1. **Start Condition:** The Master pulls SDA low while SCL is high. This wakes up all 127 Slaves.
2. **The Address:** The Master sends 7 bits representing the unique address of the target Slave (e.g., `0x68` for a gyroscope), followed by 1 bit indicating Read (1) or Write (0).
3. **ACK (Acknowledge):** The Master releases the SDA line. The target Slave (and only the target Slave) must pull SDA low to say "I'm here!" (ACK). If the line stays high, it's a NACK (Nobody responded).
4. **Data:** Data is transmitted in 8-bit chunks. After every 8 bits, the receiving device must issue an ACK.
5. **Stop Condition:** The Master releases SCL, then releases SDA, returning the bus to Idle.

## 4. Master vs Slave (Multi-Master)
I2C is technically a Multi-Master bus. Unlike SPI, you can have two microcontrollers on the same bus both trying to act as Masters. If they start transmitting at exactly the same time, I2C uses **Clock Stretching** and **Arbitration** to safely decide who wins the bus (the one who sends a `0` first wins).
